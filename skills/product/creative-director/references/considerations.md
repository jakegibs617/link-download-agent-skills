# Creative Director Review — Full Question List

Questions per focus area. Answer from repo evidence; note explicitly when the
repo is silent — silence is a gradeable finding, not a gap to work around.

**These questions grade the idea, not the craft.** Several of them can be
answered impressively by a project with excellent execution and no point of view;
that project grades 🔴. Where a question asks what the brand *refuses* or *gives
up*, an inability to answer is the finding, and it does not become a pass because
the assets are beautiful.

Interface quality is out of scope throughout — read the design system for its
point of view, and leave whether the screens work to `ui-ux-plan`.

## Brand idea & positioning

- What is the one sentence this brand owns? Could the nearest competitor put it on their site unchanged — and if so, it isn't positioning.
- What does the brand deliberately give up? (A position with no cost is a description.)
- Is there a single organizing idea, or several competing ones across the docs?
- What's the promise to the audience, and does the product as scoped actually keep it?
- If the logo and name were removed, would anyone know whose this is?

## Creative concept

- Is there a campaign-able idea — something expressible as a line, a film, and a still — or only a feature list in marketing clothes?
- Does the idea extend past one execution? Name three executions it could produce.
- Does it survive compression: a 6-second cut, a single static frame, an app-store thumbnail?
- What's the hook in the first two seconds, and what makes someone stop scrolling?
- Is the concept borrowed from the category's current trend, and what happens when the trend turns over?

## Visual identity & art direction

- What are the three non-negotiable visual rules? Are they written down anywhere or only implied by assets?
- What does the identity *refuse* to look like? (No refusal = no direction.)
- Is color, type, and imagery a system with intent, or framework defaults and stock choices?
- Does the art direction express the brand idea, or merely decorate it?
- Would a new designer produce on-brand work from what exists? What would they get wrong?
- Where does the identity break — an asset, a screen, a surface that clearly came from elsewhere?

## Voice & copy

- Read the user-facing copy aloud: whose voice is this? Name a person or archetype, or admit it's nobody's.
- Is there a written voice do/don't, and does the actual microcopy obey it?
- Quote the best line in the project and the worst. What's the gap between them?
- Does the copy say what the product *is*, or what it *does for me*?
- Is the naming (product, features, currencies, modes) doing brand work or just labeling?
- Does the voice hold in the unglamorous places — errors, empty states, permissions, legal?

## Audience resonance & cultural fit

- Who is this for, specifically enough to picture one person? Who is it explicitly not for?
- What does this audience already love, and what does the brand borrow credibility from?
- What's the shareable moment — the thing a user screenshots or sends to a friend, unprompted?
- What cultural context or subculture does this live inside, and does the creative speak its language or explain it from outside?
- Is there any real signal of resonance (community, comps, research), or only internal conviction?
- What could read as tone-deaf, derivative, or offensive to this audience?

## Craft & consistency

- Do the name, key art, store copy, onboarding, and in-product tone tell the same story?
- Where does polish visibly stop — which surface was clearly finished last or not at all?
- Is there a first-impression path (ad → store page → first 60 seconds) and does it hold one idea end to end?
- What is the weakest asset a user will actually see, and is it load-bearing?
- Are there placeholders, temp art, or lorem copy still in user-facing paths?
- If shipped tomorrow, what would the CD be embarrassed by?
